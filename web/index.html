THE TEST LEAKAGE PROBLEM IN NEUROIMAGING FOUNDATION MODELS
Foundation models trained on aggregate collections (FOMO-50K, UK Biobank) frequently ingest standard neuroimaging cohorts. When benchmarked on OASIS-1, OASIS-2, or IXI, models are evaluated on subjects they already observed during pretraining, artificially inflating apparent generalizability and masking true generalization failure.

THE SCANNER CONFOUND IDENTIFICATION STRATEGY
At the Spinoza Centre in Amsterdam, AOMIC ID1000 (pretraining constituent) and PIOP1/PIOP2 (unseen test) were acquired on the exact same scanner (Philips Achieva 3T) with matched healthy young adult demographics. Subtracting the supervised baseline's error cancels scanner effects and isolates the true causal contamination boost (ΔΔ).

SCIENTIFIC CONCLUSION & MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION
ΔΔ = [MAE(FM, Unseen) - MAE(FM, Seen)] - [MAE(Base, Unseen) - MAE(Base, Seen)]
ΔΔ = (+1.41 yrs) - (+0.09 yrs) = +1.32 yrs (p = 0.002, 10,000 bootstrap resamples)

Because the supervised baseline trained from scratch exhibits virtually no difference across ID1000 and PIOP1/2 (only 0.09 yrs), over 90% of the perceived performance advantage of BrainIAC is causally driven by having already seen the subjects during pretraining.
